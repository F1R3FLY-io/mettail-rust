# The Rho Calculus in GSLT Form

A **Graph-Structured Lambda Theory (GSLT)** presents a language as a triple
$\langle \text{Syntax},\; \text{Equations},\; \text{Rewrites} \rangle$:

1. a free syntax (the grammar of terms),
2. a set of equations identifying terms that should be considered the same,
3. a set of rewrite rules giving the operational dynamics modulo those equations.

What follows is the rho calculus in exactly this form.

---

## 1. Free Syntax

The rho calculus is built from two mutually recursive sorts: **processes** ($P, Q$) and **names** ($x, y$). Names are reified — quoted — processes; this *reflection* is what makes rho a higher-order, name-passing calculus without an external set of channel atoms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P, Q &::=\; 0 \;\mid\; \mathtt{for}(\, y \Leftarrow x \,)\,P \;\mid\; x!(\, Q \,) \;\mid\; P \mid Q \;\mid\; {*}x \\[4pt]
x, y &::=\; @P
\end{aligned}
$$

The five process constructors:

- $0$ — the **stopped** (inert) process.
- $\mathtt{for}(\, y \Leftarrow x \,)\,P$ — **input**: receive a name on channel $x$, bind it to $y$ in the continuation $P$.
- $x!(\, Q \,)$ — **output**: send the process $Q$ on channel $x$.
- $P \mid Q$ — **parallel composition** of $P$ and $Q$.
- ${*}x$ — **dereference** (drop): unquote the name $x$ back into a running process.

And on the name side:

- $@P$ — **reflection** (quote): turn a process $P$ into a name $@P$.

The two operators $@\,\_$ and ${*}\,\_$ are inverses up to structural equivalence: $*@P =_S P$.

---

## 2. Equations

Let $\equiv_S$ denote **structural equivalence**. It is defined as the smallest equivalence relation on process terms that satisfies the following:

- **Alpha equivalence.** Terms that differ only by the choice of bound name are identified. The sole binder is the input prefix: in $\mathtt{for}(\, y \Leftarrow x \,)\,P$, the name $y$ on the left of the arrow is bound in $P$.

- **Commutative monoid laws for parallel composition.** $(P,\, \mid,\, 0)$ is a commutative monoid. Concretely:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P \mid 0 &\equiv_S P                       &&\text{(identity)} \\
P \mid Q &\equiv_S Q \mid P                 &&\text{(commutativity)} \\
(P \mid Q) \mid R &\equiv_S P \mid (Q \mid R) &&\text{(associativity)}
\end{aligned}
$$

These equations give the calculus its "soup of processes" semantics: parallel composition has no preferred ordering or grouping, and $0$ contributes nothing.

---

## 3. Rewrite Rules

The reduction relation $\rightsquigarrow$ is generated by the following three rules.

### COMM — Communication

$$
\mathtt{for}(\, y \Leftarrow x \,)\,P \;\mid\; x!(\, Q \,) \;\rightsquigarrow\; P\{\, @Q\, /\, y \,\}
$$

An input on $x$ meets an output on the same $x$. The transmitted process $Q$ is **quoted** to the name $@Q$, and that name is substituted for the bound $y$ throughout the continuation $P$. Note that what travels across the channel is a *name* — the reified form of $Q$ — not the process itself; this is the defining trick of the rho calculus.

### PAR — Parallel Context

$$
\frac{P \rightsquigarrow P'}{P \mid Q \;\rightsquigarrow\; P' \mid Q}
$$

Reduction is closed under parallel composition: an inert sibling does not block a redex.

### STRUCT — Reduction Modulo Structural Equivalence

$$
\frac{P \equiv_S P' \quad P' \rightsquigarrow Q' \quad Q' \equiv_S Q}{P \;\rightsquigarrow\; Q}
$$

We may apply structural equivalence freely before and after a reduction step. This is what lets COMM fire when the input and output are not syntactically adjacent — the monoid laws can rearrange them into position.

---

## 4. Reading the Triple

Putting it all together, the rho calculus *is* the triple

$$
\Big\langle\; \text{the BNF for } P, Q, x, y \;,\;\; \equiv_S \;,\;\; \rightsquigarrow \;\Big\rangle
$$

— a grammar, an equational theory making it a quotient, and a rewrite relation on that quotient. Every rho calculus computation is a sequence of COMM steps lifted through PAR and reorganized via STRUCT.

This GSLT presentation is exactly the shape MeTTaIL takes as input: a language defined by syntactic types, equations on types, and rewrites on types. The rho calculus is then both the canonical example and the host language in which other GSLTs can be embedded.
